A look at the first publications of 2025 that include lab members and collaborators.

- **[Absolute abundance unveils *Basidiobolus* as a cross-domain bridge indirectly bolstering gut microbiome homeostasis](https://doi.org/10.1093/ismejo/wraf150)** (*ISME Journal*). A collaboration led by Mitra Ghotbi and Donald Walker at Middle Tennessee State University, with [Kian Kelly](/members/kian-kelly), [Leila Shadmani](/members/leila-shadmani), [Julissa Perez-Marron](/members/julissa-perez-marron), Joey Spatafora, and Jason Stajich. The study applies absolute abundance profiling, using the DspikeIn framework, to bacterial and fungal gut microbiomes across amphibians and reptiles. It identifies core taxa that relative abundance hides, and suggests *Basidiobolus* acts as a connector between microbial modules in the gut network.
- **[Fungal impacts on Earth's ecosystems](https://doi.org/10.1038/s41586-024-08419-4)** (*Nature*). A broad perspective with 33 authors on the roles fungi play across Earth's ecosystems.
- **[Section-level genome sequencing and comparative genomics of *Aspergillus* sections *Cavernicolus* and *Usti*](https://doi.org/10.3114/sim.2025.111.03)** (*Studies in Mycology*). A genome sequencing and comparative genomics study of two sections of the genus *Aspergillus*.
- **[Assessing the Evolutionary Trajectory of Arbuscular Mycorrhizal Conserved Genes in Seagrasses and Aquatic Close Relatives](https://doi.org/10.1101/2025.01.23.634575)** (bioRxiv preprint). [Cassandra Ettinger](/members/cassie-ettinger), Jennifer Arroyo, and Jason Stajich examine whether genes conserved for arbuscular mycorrhizal symbiosis are retained in seagrasses and their aquatic relatives.
